LESSON 1 / EMPATHIZE

Think like your audience

Understand the decisions, concerns and language of the people in the room.

Why this step matters

Understand what people think, feel and value before deciding what they need. Listening and observing help you separate their real concerns from your assumptions.

For example Before explaining automation, ask an operations leader which handoffs cause the most frustration. Their answer may change the story you planned to tell.

LESSON 2 / DEFINE

Decide what the story must achieve

Turn a broad presentation topic into a precise problem and a desired decision.

Why this step matters

Bring your observations together and name the problem clearly. A useful problem statement connects the current situation with the understanding or decision you want to create.

For example “Explain automation” is a topic. “Help leaders decide which manual process to improve first” gives your story a purpose.

2A · Ask why five times

Start with: “We need to explain how automation is being used.” Ask why until you reach the real decision, understanding or behaviour the story should create.

LESSON 3 / IDEATE

Create five different explanations

Widen your options before choosing a storyline.

Why this step matters

Explore different possibilities before judging them. Generating several ideas gives you options to compare and combine instead of committing to the first familiar answer.

For example Try telling the same story through an employee’s day, a before-and-after process and a business outcome. Then compare what each makes clear.

LESSON 4 / PROTOTYPE

Build a memorable mental model

Make the story tangible with a small set of connected business capabilities.

Why this step matters

Make your idea tangible with something quick and inexpensive, such as a sketch or storyboard. A prototype helps people react to the idea while it is still easy to change.

For example Write one headline and one example on each of eight slide cards. Rearrange the cards to try a different story before designing the slides.

LESSON 5 / TEST

Let management challenge the story

Test the logic and language before polishing the deck.

Why this step matters

Find out whether your idea works for the people who need it. Test assumptions, listen to feedback and revise the explanation—or revisit the problem when needed.

For example Ask a colleague to repeat your story back. If they confuse an agent with rules-based automation, revise the explanation and try again.

5A · Five-minute management simulation

Presenter: deliver the storyboard in five minutes. Reviewer: interrupt naturally and note where the explanation becomes vague, technical or unsupported.
